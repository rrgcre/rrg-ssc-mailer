Listings — Help & User Guide

A listing file is one home for a single engagement — a business you're selling, or a space you're leasing. From this one screen you set the terms, move it through the pipeline, put a blind teaser on the marketplace, work the buyers, log NDAs, tours and offers, open the deal when it goes under contract, and bill the fee. This guide walks the screen top to bottom.

1 Overview

The listing file is laid out in three columns, the same way the Contact and Company detail pages are.

  • Left — Manage. The controls for the listing: status, pipelines, owner, the seller/client contact, agreement dates, and marketing IDs. Everything here autosaves.
  • Middle — the listing at a glance. Listing facts, the seller pipeline, the buyer pipeline, and Publish to Marketplace.
  • Right — the working rail. Buyer leads, matching buyers, the deal, NDAs, tours, offers, agreements, expenses, invoices, and data-room activity — cards you can reorder and collapse to fit how you work.

A listing starts life as a seller record or a qualification call and becomes a live listing once financials are verified, value is aligned, and the agreement is signed — use Promote to Live listing in Manage when it's ready.

2 The Manage panel

The fixed left column is the cockpit for the listing. It stays put while the rest of the page scrolls, and every field saves on its own.

  • Status — where the listing stands (New, Active, Under Contract, Closed, On Hold, Lost).
  • Pipeline — which board the listing sits on and its stage (see Seller pipeline).
  • Buyer pipeline — the set of stages your buyer leads move through (see Buyer pipeline).
  • Owner — the assigned rep.
  • Seller contact — the client. Type a name to link it to the contact record; once linked, it becomes a one-click link on the listing.
  • Referred by / Referral % — the source of the deal and any referral fee owed.
  • Business name & Market — how the listing is labeled and where it trades.
  • Listing agreement — listed-on, expiration, live (on-market) date, days-on-market, and auto-renew. The panel warns you as the agreement nears expiry.
  • Listing IDs & lead routing — see Lead routing.
  • Notes — private notes on the listing: next steps, seller temperature, key dates.
Autosaves
Fields commit when you leave them or after a short pause while typing — there's no separate save step for most edits.

3 Seller pipeline

The seller pipeline tracks the listing's own progress from screening to close.

The board is set in Admin → Pipelines and its stages show as a horizontal tracker in the middle column — done stages fill green, the current one is marked in progress. Pick which board a listing sits on from the Pipeline field in Manage. Some stages are automatic (they light up as the underlying work happens); others are manual — mark them done when you reach them.

4 Buyer pipeline

The buyer pipeline is the buyer-side mirror of the seller pipeline — it's how you track the buyers working this listing.

A buyer pipeline is any pipeline whose Practice area is set to Buyer in Admin → Pipelines. Pick one from the Buyer pipeline field in Manage, and its stages become the funnel every buyer lead on this listing moves through. The Buyer Pipeline board in the middle column (tinted a light shade of your brand red so it reads distinct from the seller pipeline) shows each stage with a live count of how many buyers sit in it. You move an individual buyer through the stages from the stage dropdown on their lead in Buyer Leads.

Setting one up
If no buyer pipeline exists yet, Manage shows a link to create one. In Admin → Pipelines, add a pipeline, set its Practice area to Buyer, and add your stages (e.g. Contacted → Qualified → NDA Sent → Toured → Offer).

5 Publish to Marketplace

Put the listing in front of buyers as a blind teaser without giving away who it is.

Flip Public on and the listing appears on your confidential buyer marketplace — but the business name, address, and seller stay hidden until a buyer signs an NDA. You control what buyers see: a blind headline, category/concept, market and location label, price guide, and the revenue / cash-flow bands. Mark it Featured to push it to the top. Characterizing the teaser (concept, market, or price) is also what powers buyer matching, so fill it in even before you go public.

Publishing pushes the deal to matching buyers automatically. The moment you go public, the system checks every active buyer in your book against the teaser and, for each match, logs it on that buyer's timeline and drops a task in your list — "N buyers match this listing — send the teaser under NDA." Publish again later and only new matches are flagged, so no buyer is ever pinged twice. The Save button also tells you how many matched on the spot.

6 Buyer leads & matching

Two cards work together on the right rail: the buyers who've raised their hand, and the buyers in your book who should.

Buyer Leads is every buyer inquiry on this listing — imported from BizBuySell (the ⬇ Import leads button on the Listings page) or added by hand. Each lead is tracked and carries a stage dropdown so you can move it through the buyer pipeline; the card summarizes how many buyers sit in each stage.

Matching buyers surfaces the active buyers from your book whose buy-box fits this listing — with the reasons each matched, plus one-click Open and Email. Matches appear once the listing is characterized in Publish to Marketplace, so the moment a deal is ready you know exactly who to call.

You don't have to check this card to catch a match, though: publishing the teaser proactively flags every matching buyer on their timeline and drops a task in your list, so new inventory reaches the right buyers without anyone remembering to look.

7 NDAs, tours & offers

The steps between "interested" and "under contract," each logged as it happens.

  • NDAs — record each signed NDA so you know exactly who is cleared to see the CIM and enter the data room.
  • Location Tours — log each site visit: who came, when, who hosted, and their read — so you can see interest building across the marketing period.
  • Offers — IOIs & LOIs — capture each offer as it lands (amount, buyer, terms), rate it, and let the RRG analyst score it for you.

8 Deal, expenses & invoices

Once a buyer's committed, the listing carries the transaction to close and bills the fee.

Open the Deal when a buyer goes under contract — buyer, price, and close date — to track it to closing. Deal Expenses records costs tied to the listing, and Invoices lets you bill your commission with the invoice filed against the listing. Signed Agreements generated for the listing collect on the rail here too.

9 Financials, value & pricing

Top and bottom line in one place — and controlled, tracked pricing.

  • Snapshot — asking price, opinion of value, and the price multiple (asking ÷ the latest year's earnings) across the top of the Financials card.
  • Financials — revenue, SDE, EBITDA and rent for the last three years plus a TTM (trailing-twelve) column. Edit a period header if your fiscal year differs. These are the numbers a buyer asks for first.
  • From the BOV, or by hand — when a valuation (BOV) exists, opinion of value and basis flow from it automatically. With no BOV, type them right here so the listing is never blank. Finalizing a BOV also writes its TTM revenue / SDE / EBITDA back onto this grid.
  • The lease drives the value — so the BOV asks for it first. On a leased restaurant or bar, before building a valuation the system looks for a lease abstract: if one's on file it builds straight through and folds the lease terms (remaining term, options, occupancy cost as a % of sales, assignability, guaranty) into the analysis; if a lease is on file but not abstracted it offers to abstract it first; if there's no lease at all it asks whether to proceed without one. The abstract's Occupancy & Economics section computes occupancy cost as a % of sales live — the single best read on whether the rent is healthy.
  • Changing the asking price is a controlled action — it's not free-typed. Whoever holds the Change asking price permission (your marketing team) uses the Change button to set a new price and an optional reason; everyone else sees the price read-only.
  • Every change is tracked — each price change is logged with its date, the previous price, and the reason, building a price history right on the listing.

The pricing permission only takes effect once an admin turns on Enforce permissions and grants the Change asking price right to the marketing role. Until then, anyone who can edit the listing can change the price.

10 Data room activity

See which buyers are actually digging in.

The Data Room Activity card shows who accessed the room, who signed in with an access code, and what they downloaded — a running read on buyer engagement. Use Open room → to jump into the room itself to add buyers, share the link, or manage documents.

11 Seller activity report

An automatic activity summary that keeps the owner informed — the cheapest way to hold a listing and win the renewal.

The Seller activity report emails each seller a running summary of the marketing on their listing — buyer inquiries, NDAs signed, tours, offers, and confidential data-room views over the period — so they always know you're working. Sellers go quiet and cancel when they feel like nothing's happening; this is what prevents that.

The cadence is a firm setting in Admin → Email → Weekly seller activity report: turn it on and choose the interval — every 7, 14, or 30 days (14 is the default; 7 can be too frequent). Once it's on, the report goes out automatically for every listing that's live on the marketplace and has a seller email on file.

Per listing, you're in control from the Seller activity report card on the right rail: it shows the current period's numbers, who it sends to, and when it last went out. Send now fires one immediately, and Skip this listing opts a single deal out of the firm cadence. The email is strictly confidential — it never reveals the business name, address, or any buyer's identity — and it has a graceful "quieter stretch" version for weeks with no new activity.

12 Lead routing & marketing IDs

Tie your outside listing IDs to this record so inbound buyer leads attach themselves automatically.

In Manage, set the BizBuySell Ref ID and #, CoStar / LoopNet #, and Crexi # for the listing. Imported buyer leads that carry those IDs then auto-attach to the right listing. You can also pick an automation to run on every lead that comes in for this listing — an instant follow-up, a task, a tag — so nothing sits.

13 Make the layout yours

The right rail is yours to arrange — just like the Contact and Company detail pages.

Grab any card by its ⠿ handle and drag it up or down to reorder, and use the ▾ chevron to collapse the ones you don't need. Your order and which cards are collapsed are saved on this device, so the listing opens the way you left it. The Manage panel on the left and the overview cards in the middle stay put.

14 Lease & tenant-rep listings

The same file runs a tenant-rep or landlord engagement, with the fields that fit that work.

On a tenant-rep listing, the marketing-IDs box is replaced by the site criteria — markets, use type, budget, size range, term, timeline, parking, and required features — the same vocabulary the tenant-box uses to match spaces.

On a landlord-rep listing (started from Landlord Rep → Space Listings), that box becomes Space for lease — size, asking rent, lease term, condition (2nd-gen restaurant, vanilla shell, …), suitable use, available date, and key features — the space you're marketing on the landlord's behalf. The contact is the landlord, the asking-price and business-financials cards don't apply, and you work tenant prospects (inquiries, tours, LOIs) instead of buyers. The pipeline, agreement dates, expenses, invoices, and reorderable rail all work the same way.

15 FAQ

I don't see a Buyer pipeline to pick.

None exists yet. In Admin → Pipelines, create a pipeline and set its Practice area to Buyer, then add your stages. It'll show in the Buyer pipeline field on every listing.

Where do I add a buyer lead?

On the Buyer Leads card in the right rail — the + button. Or import buyers in bulk from BizBuySell using the Import leads button on the Listings page; leads carrying this listing's marketing IDs attach automatically.

Why don't any matching buyers show?

Matching needs the listing characterized — set its concept, market, or price in Publish to Marketplace. Only active buyers (buy-box toggled on) are matched.

My rail keeps resetting.

Layout is saved per device in this browser. A different browser, a private window, or cleared site data starts from the default order. Drag it back and it'll stick again on that device.

How do I take a listing live?

Use Promote to Live listing in Manage once financials are verified, value is aligned, and the agreement is signed.

The seller isn't getting the activity report.

Three things have to be true: the firm cadence is on in Admin → Email → Weekly seller activity report, the listing is live on the marketplace, and the listing has a seller email on file (set the seller contact in Manage). Also confirm outbound email itself is configured on that same Admin → Email page. You can always use Send now on the listing's Seller activity report card to test it, and Skip this listing to opt one deal out.

The BOV asked me to add a lease before valuing — why?

On a leased restaurant or bar the lease drives the value (remaining term, options, occupancy cost as a % of sales, assignability), so the valuation looks for a lease abstract first. If one's on file it just builds and folds those terms in; if not, it offers to abstract the lease first, or lets you proceed without one. You're never blocked — but the number is far more defensible with the lease read.
