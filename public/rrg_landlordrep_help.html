Landlord Rep — Help & User Guide

Landlord Rep is where you represent a property owner leasing restaurant or bar space. You list the available space, market it, field tenant inquiries, run tours and LOIs, and negotiate to a signed lease — one file per space, from listing to executed.

1 Overview

Space Listings is the home for every landlord engagement you're running.

The list shows each space you're marketing with its landlord, market, size, asking rent, and status. Search across the top to find one fast, then click a space name to open its listing file — the same one-page HQ your business-sale listings use, adapted for a lease.

One engine, two sides
A landlord space listing shares the pipeline, tours, LOIs, documents and data room with the rest of the platform — so you're not learning a second system, just working the other side of the deal.

2 Start a space listing

Three fields to open a new engagement.

Click + New space listing and give it a space / listing name (e.g. "Former Cantina — 1200 S Congress"), the market (from your admin market list), and the landlord contact. Creating it links the landlord to your registry, builds the listing's data room, drops it on the Landlord pipeline, and opens the listing so you can fill in the space details.

3 Space details

The space you're marketing, captured on the listing HQ.

On the listing's Manage panel, the Space for lease box holds everything a prospective tenant asks first:

  • Size (SF) and asking rent — e.g. "$32/SF NNN" or "$6,500/mo."
  • Lease term available and available date.
  • Condition — 2nd-generation restaurant, vanilla shell, gray/cold shell, as-is, or other.
  • Suitable use and key features — hood & grease trap, patio, liquor-eligible, parking, drive-thru.
  • Space notes — landlord terms, TI allowance, co-tenants, restrictions.

The listing's contact is the landlord, and the business-sale financials, asking-price and marketplace cards don't apply — this is real estate for lease, not a business for sale.

4 The landlord pipeline

Move each space from listed to leased.

Every space sits on the Landlord Rep pipeline and moves through its stages: Listing Setup → Marketing → Tours → LOI Received → Lease Negotiation → Executed. Work the board the same way as your sale listings — the stage is the deal's status at a glance.

5 Tenant prospects

The same deal machinery, pointed at tenants instead of buyers.

On the listing HQ you log every inquiry from a prospective tenant, record each tour of the space, and capture each LOI as it comes in — building the record of interest across the marketing period. Use the LOI Builder to draft a letter of intent with the landlord and tenant terms filled in.

6 Documents & data room

Every space's paper in one place.

The listing carries its own documents and a confidential data room — put the site plan, floor plan, current lease or LOI, and any exhibits here so the whole file lives together and can be shared with a prospective tenant when the time is right.

7 FAQ

How is this different from Tenant Rep?

Tenant Rep represents the tenant hunting for space (site criteria, space search). Landlord Rep represents the landlord marketing a space for lease (the space listing, tenant prospects). Same platform, opposite sides.

Where do I set the markets I can pick?

Markets come from your admin market list (Admin → Settings → Markets), the same list every other section uses.

Can I still use tours and LOIs?

Yes — they're shared across the platform. Tours and LOIs on a landlord listing work exactly like they do on a business-sale listing.
