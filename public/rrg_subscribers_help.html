Subscribers — Help & User Guide

Subscribers is your marketing audience — the people who receive mass emails — kept in its own list, separate from your CRM contacts. Everyone carries a type, areas of interest, status and preferences, so you can slice the database into segments and send the right campaign to the right people.

1 Overview

One list, built to be sliced.

The four tiles up top show the size of your audience — total, subscribed, area-only, and unsubscribed. Below them is the list itself, with each subscriber's name, email, company, type, areas, status and the date they were added. The filters across the top of the list are how you segment; click any name to open that person's detail page.

2 Segment the database

Narrow the list to exactly who you'd send a campaign to.

Three filters sit across the top of the list, and they stack:

  • Type — Broker, Restaurant, or Buyer.
  • Area — any of your markets (San Antonio, Austin, Dallas, Houston…), drawn from your admin market list.
  • Status — Subscribed or Unsubscribed.

Add the search box and you can find anyone by name, email or company. Whatever the list shows after filtering is a segment — e.g. "Buyers interested in Austin who are subscribed."

Why it matters
A restaurant-for-sale blast should go to Buyers, not Brokers; an Austin listing should go to people who care about Austin. Segmenting first means every send is relevant — better opens, fewer unsubscribes.

3 The fields you track

Enough to know who someone is and what to send them.

  • First name / Last name and Email — who they are.
  • Company — where they're from.
  • Type — Broker, Restaurant, or Buyer — the primary way you segment.
  • Areas of interest — all areas, or specific markets they care about.
  • Status — Subscribed or Unsubscribed.
  • Date added — when they entered the list.
  • Tags — free-form labels for any other slicing you want.

4 The subscriber detail page

A dedicated page to manage one person's marketing profile.

Click a subscriber's name to open their detail page. There you set the first/last name, email, company, type, status, areas of interest (all areas, or pick specific ones), and tags. Save keeps the changes and Delete removes them from the audience. Use Back to list to return to the segment you were working.

5 Add & import

Three ways to grow the list.

  • + Add subscriber — open a blank detail page and enter one by hand.
  • Import → Paste a list — paste emails one per line (optionally email, name, company) and apply tags to the batch.
  • Import → From contacts — pull people straight from your CRM contacts, filtered by contact type or tag, so your book and your audience stay in sync.

6 Email preferences

Each subscriber controls what they get — and the system honors it.

On the detail page, Areas of interest is either All areas (they get every campaign) or Specific areas (they only get a campaign that overlaps the areas you picked). Anyone marked Unsubscribed is never emailed. Every mass email respects these settings automatically, so you never have to hand-clean a list before a send.

7 Feeds Bulk Email

The segment you build here is who a campaign reaches.

Subscribers is the audience layer under Bulk Email. When you build and send a campaign, it targets subscribers by the same type / area / status you segment on here — and honors each person's preferences and unsubscribe. Keep this list clean and well-typed, and your campaigns land where they should.

8 FAQ

How is this different from Contacts?

Contacts is your CRM — everyone you work with. Subscribers is your marketing audience — the subset who get mass emails, with their own preferences and unsubscribe. Import pulls contacts in; the two stay separate lists.

Where do the areas come from?

From your admin market list (Admin → Settings → Markets), the same list every section uses.

Do unsubscribed people ever get emailed?

No. Unsubscribed is a hard stop — they're excluded from every send automatically.
