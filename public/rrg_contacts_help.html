Contacts — Help & User Guide

A contact is one record per person in your book — a buyer, a seller, a landlord, an advisor. Every contact is tied to the company they work for and to every deal, document, and conversation they touch, so the whole relationship is visible in one place. This guide covers what a contact record does and how to get the most out of it.

Book of BusinessOne record per personLinked to everything

1 Overview

Contacts live under Book of Business → Contacts. Each row is a person; open one to see their identity, how to reach them, their company, and every record they're connected to. New contacts come in three ways: added by hand with + New contact, imported in bulk from a CRM, or created automatically as you work a deal.

2 Linked to their company

Every contact ties to the operator or business they belong to, so you never work a person in isolation.

On the contact you'll see the company with a direct open → link; on the company you'll see all its contacts. Links are made by email domain where possible (the reliable way — see the import and the Data tools' email-domain matcher), so a person at @copperforkholdings.com attaches to Copper Fork Holdings without guessing on the name. A contact with no company sits on the catch-all No Company record until you link it.

Main contact
The primary (main) contact is the go-to person for a company — starred on the company card. When a company has just one contact, the system makes that person the main automatically.

3 Every touch tracked

The contact is the timeline of your relationship, so nothing goes cold and nothing gets lost.

  • Email — with Gmail connected, sent and received mail threads show right on the contact, and you can send from inside the record.
  • Calls & notes — log a call or drop a note; a note written as a to-do becomes a task.
  • Activity feed — a running, filterable history of every interaction and change on the record.
  • Last contacted — a stamp that tells you at a glance who's overdue for a touch, and that you can filter and sort by.

4 Flags that matter

Fast, visual triage of your book. Toggle a flag on the contact and filter by it on the list.

  • VIP — your most important relationships.
  • Star — a personal shortlist / follow-up mark.
  • Bad news (caution) — flag a difficult party or bad actor so the whole team is warned.
  • Primary contact — the main person for their company (see Linked to their company).

5 Interests & lead source

Categorize who someone is and where they came from, then work the list by segment.

Main interests classify the contact — buying, selling, investor, landlord, tenant, and so on (the list is admin-editable, so it fits how your firm works). Lead source records where the relationship originated — referral, website, CoStar, LoopNet, an event, a cold call. Both are filterable, so you can pull, say, every selling contact from a referral in Austin in seconds.

6 Buyer buy-box & matching

Turn your buyer list into a matching engine — capture what each buyer wants, then see the listings (or buyers) that fit.

On a buyer's contact, the Buy-Box captures their acquisition criteria in the same vocabulary as your listings: target concepts, markets, a max price, a minimum SDE, a unit-count range, whether they need real estate, and their timeline & financing. Flip the Active toggle on when they're genuinely looking, and the card lists the current listings that match — with the reasons each one matched.

It works both ways. On any listing, the Matching buyers card shows the active buyers in your book whose buy-box fits that deal, with one-click Open and Email — so the moment a deal is ready you know exactly who to call. A listing only appears as a match once it's been characterized (its concept, market, or price set in Publish-to-Marketplace), so the matches always mean something.

7 Everything they touch

The contact collects every record the person is part of, so the full picture is one screen.

Listings and assignments, agreements (with sign status), LOIs, valuations and BOVs, marketing packs, tours, NDAs, inquiries, appointments, and uploaded documents (leases, P&Ls, IDs) all surface on the contact — each a click from where it lives. When you generate an agreement or a valuation for someone, it files itself onto their record automatically.

Relationship value at a glance

Above the activity feed, the record rolls up the money side of the relationship as quick tiles: Active Listings (open listings where they're the client), Closed Deals (transactions marked closed, whether they were the seller or the buyer), and Commission generated from those closed deals — with pipeline value shown on hover. It's the fastest way to tell a repeat, revenue-producing client from a name you've never closed with. The company record carries the same roll-up across all of its listings.

8 Seller screening & interviews

Qualify sellers and capture their story right from the contact.

Start a seller screening call to run a structured qualification — concept, numbers, lease, motivation — and, when it moves forward, spin it into a listing or a data room without re-typing. Send a seller interview to have the seller answer a set of questions on camera at their own pace; the video and transcript attach for your team's reference. Both live on the contact and flow into the deal.

9 Headshots

A photo on the contact so your team recognizes who they're calling or meeting.

Upload a headshot on the contact record; it then shows on the contact, on the company's contact list, and anywhere the person appears. Small touch, real payoff when a rep is walking into a meeting or picking up the phone.

10 Import from anywhere

Bring your people in from your old CRM in a few clicks — nothing gets lost or doubled.

Under Settings → Data → Import Data, upload a Copper, HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Gmail or plain CSV/Excel export. The importer maps your columns, then dedupes by email and links each contact to its company automatically as it runs. Every import is a numbered batch you can revert in one click. You can also pull buyers straight from BizBuySell and contacts from Gmail using the buttons on the Contacts page.

11 Filters & saved searches

Slice your book any way you work and save the views you run most.

Use ⚙ Filters to narrow by main interest, owner, lead source, tags, flags, and created / last-contacted dates — each a type-to-search chip field, so even long lists are a couple of keystrokes. Name and save a search (just for you or shared with the team) and it's one click next time. The list also focuses on recently-active records by default, with a Show all toggle.

12 FAQ

A contact isn't linked to a company.

Open the contact and set the company in the Company field, or run the email-domain matcher under Settings → Data → Data Enrichment to link loose contacts in bulk by their email domain.

How do I set the main contact for a company?

If a company has one contact, it's set automatically. For several, open the company and star the person you want as main — or toggle the Primary flag on the contact.

My imported contacts show as duplicates.

Import dedupes by email, but contacts imported without an email can double up. Use Find Duplicates under Settings → Data to merge them without losing history.

Where do emails come from on the contact?

Connect Gmail under Settings → Integrations. Once connected, mail threads with that contact appear on their record and you can send from there.
